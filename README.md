## Overview

This repository contains the field research, patient case series, clinical questionnaire, and analytical report for a multi-regional study on **Clubfoot** (Congenital Talipes Equinovarus — CTEV) in Bangladesh. 

The study was conducted by undergraduate researchers from the **Department of Genetic Engineering and Biotechnology, Shahjalal University of Science and Technology**, Sylhet, under the Cytology course curriculum (**GEB 133**).

### Abstract

Clubfoot is a congenital foot deformity characterised by medial deviation of the heel, forefoot adduction, and ankle inversion. While global prevalence is approximately 1 in 1,000 live births, in Bangladesh the estimated incidence is around 1 in 900 births (~5,000 children born each year with the condition).

This study documents primary clinical, familial, and demographic profiles of **16 patients** evaluated through hospital visits and door-to-door field interviews across multiple districts of Bangladesh. The investigation assesses:
- Clinical variation and deformity classification (*Equinovarus*, *Varus*, unilateral vs. bilateral).
- Therapeutic interventions (the non-invasive **Ponseti method** involving serial casting, Achilles tenotomy, and bracing vs. surgical procedures).
- Potential hereditary components and syndromic associations (chromosomal involvement such as 17q23 microdeletions/duplications, trisomies, and multi-generational recurrence).
- Socioeconomic bottlenecks, healthcare infrastructure gaps, and treatment adherence barriers affecting low-income families in Bangladesh.

## Key Findings & Cohort Summary

The study evaluated **16 clinical cases** (9 males, 7 females) spanning patients aged from 34 days to 55 years old:

```
Total Cohort (N = 16)
 ├── Sex Distribution:
 │    ├── Male: 56.25% (n = 9)
 │    └── Female: 43.75% (n = 7)
 ├── Age Range:
 │    ├── Infants & Young Children: 34 days – 3.5 years (majority)
 │    ├── Older Children / Young Adults: 8 – 22 years
 │    └── Untreated Adult Case: 55 years (chronic untreated CTEV)
 ├── Treatment Modalities:
 │    ├── Ponseti Method (Serial casting + Denis Browne / Steenbeek braces): ~87.5% (14 cases)
 │    │    └── Minor surgical Achilles tenotomy performed/planned in select cases
 │    ├── Major Surgical Correction: 12.5% (2 cases: Case 1, Case 4)
 │    └── Untreated / Abandoned: 1 case (Case 8, financial and access barriers)
 └── Familial / Genetic Factors:
      ├── Sporadic / Non-familial CTEV: 14 cases (~87.5%)
      └── Positive Family History: 2 cases (Case 8 & Case 15, showing multi-generational recurrence)
```

### Critical Insights & Barriers Identified

1. **Efficacy of the Ponseti Method**: Serial manipulation and casting followed by foot-abduction bracing was the primary and most successful treatment method observed, consistent with national and WHO guidelines.
2. **Economic Hurdles**: Custom-moulded corrective shoes/braces cost approximately \$5–\$10 per pair (with a production cost of \$3–\$5). For impoverished families, recurrent brace replacement as infants grow poses an acute financial burden leading to treatment discontinuation.
3. **Infrastructure Deficits**: Despite the *National Strategy and Action Plan for Clubfoot Care in Bangladesh* establishing 44 Ponseti clinics, only 3 operate in specialized tertiary hospitals and 24 in secondary district hospitals, leaving significant rural coverage gaps.
4. **Prenatal Care & Early Detection**: Multiple cases correlated with a lack of maternal prenatal monitoring and nutritional support during pregnancy, highlighting the urgent need for maternal healthcare education and early neonate screening.

## Key Recommendations & Future Directions

- **Expand Ponseti Clinic Network**: Upgrade sub-district (Upazila) and community-level healthcare centers with trained Ponseti practitioners to prevent diagnostic delay.
- **Subsidized Orthotic Devices**: Establish government and NGO partnerships (e.g., expanding initiatives like *Walk for Life* / *Zero Clubfoot Project*) to provide free or subsidized Steenbeek braces for underprivileged patients.
- **Genetic Counselling & Screening**: Offer genetic evaluation for complex or familial clubfoot cases to identify syndromic manifestations (e.g., 17q23 microdeletions or neuromuscular conditions like Charcot-Marie-Tooth).
- **Public Awareness Campaigns**: Conduct community outreach programs to eliminate social stigma and educate parents on the importance of seeking treatment within the first weeks of an infant's life.
